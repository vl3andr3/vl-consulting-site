VL Strategy and Projects

Cross-functional R&D Program Coordination

  1. Misalignment Across Functions

    Scientific, clinical, regulatory, and commercial teams often operate with different priorities, timelines, and definitions of success, leading to friction and slow decision-making.

  2. Lack of End-to-End Program Visibility

    Stakeholders don’t have a clear, unified view of progress, risks, dependencies, and timelines, making it difficult to anticipate delays or intervene early.

  3. Unclear Ownership and Decision-Making

    Critical activities fall through the cracks when roles, responsibilities, and decision rights are not clearly defined, especially across functions.

  4. Poorly Managed Dependencies and Risks

    Interdependencies between teams, such as preclinical → clinical → regulatory, are often under-coordinated, causing downstream delays and reactive firefighting.

  5. Inefficient Communication and Meeting Overload

    Teams spend excessive time in meetings without clear outcomes, while key updates and decisions fail to reach the right stakeholders at the right time.

Portfolio Governance / Milestone Planning

  1. Unclear Milestones and Decision Gates

    Programs lack well-defined stage gates, success criteria, and decision points, making it difficult to evaluate progress or decide when to advance, pause, or stop initiatives.

  2. Misalignment Between Strategy and Execution

    Portfolio priorities are not clearly translated into operational plans and milestones, resulting in teams working on activities that don’t fully support strategic goals.

  3. Inconsistent Governance Across Programs

    Different teams use varying approaches to planning and reporting, leading to inconsistent data, unclear comparisons, and difficulty managing the portfolio holistically.

  4. Limited Visibility Into Portfolio Health

    Leadership lacks a clear, consolidated view of program status, risks, timelines, and resource allocation, making it hard to make informed portfolio-level decisions.

  5. Resource Allocation Conflicts Across Programs

    Multiple programs compete for the same people, budget, or infrastructure, but without structured prioritization, this leads to overcommitment, delays, and inefficient use of resources.

Academic–Industry Collaboration Projects

  1. Misaligned Goals and Success Metrics

    Academic and industry partners often operate with different incentives, such as publications vs. product development, leading to conflicting priorities and unclear definitions of success.

  2. Unclear Governance and Decision-Making

    Collaborations frequently lack defined roles, responsibilities, and decision authority, resulting in slow approvals and stalled progress.

  3. Communication Gaps Across Organizations

    Differences in communication styles, timelines, and expectations create information silos, missed updates, and misinterpretation of progress.

  4. Complexity in Managing Deliverables and Timelines

    Coordinating across institutions introduces challenges in tracking milestones, aligning schedules, and ensuring timely delivery of shared outputs.

  5. Administrative and Operational Friction

    Contracts, data sharing agreements, IP considerations, and institutional processes can create delays and bottlenecks that slow scientific progress.

Temporary Program Leadership During Transitions

  1. Loss of Program Continuity

    When a key leader departs, critical knowledge, context, and momentum are lost, causing delays, confusion, and stalled execution.

  2. Unclear Ownership and Decision Authority

    Teams become uncertain about who is making decisions and setting priorities, leading to hesitation, duplicated efforts, or conflicting actions.

  3. Decreased Team Alignment and Morale

    Leadership gaps can create anxiety, disengagement, and misalignment, especially in high-pressure R&D environments.

  4. Breakdown in Stakeholder Communication

    Without a central point of coordination, updates to leadership, partners, and cross-functional teams become inconsistent or fragmented.

  5. Risk of Strategic Drift During Transition

    In the absence of strong leadership, programs may lose focus, deviate from strategic goals, or make short-term decisions that impact long-term outcomes.
